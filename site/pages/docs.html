Documentation

Zubi has two parts: the app on your Mac, which shares the Reminders lists, calendars and Notes folders you choose, and zubi, the client on your other devices. They meet at a relay, end-to-end encrypted, wherever each one is.

Quick start

  1. Install Zubi on your Mac. Download it, unzip it, move it to Applications and open it. Settings opens on Connection, which turns green once Zubi is connected. Zubi starts with your Mac from then on.
  2. Choose what to share. Under Reminders, Calendars and Notes, click Grant Access and set each list, calendar and folder to Read, or Read & Write where your devices may add and edit.
  3. Pair a device. On the device, install the client and run it: 
    curl -fsSL https://zubi.fa-krug.de/install.sh | sh
zubi
    It asks for a pairing code: on the Mac, choose Copy Pairing Code in Zubi’s menu, or copy it under Settings › Connection, and paste it. The client pairs, sets up the service that keeps it connected, and shows its status. Then try zubi reminders.

For more devices, add a client for each under Settings › Clients and pair it with that client’s code.

How Zubi fits together

The Mac app

Runs in the menu bar and starts with your Mac. It works with Reminders, Calendar and Notes with your permission, and answers your devices for the parts you chose to share. It accepts no connections from the internet: it connects out to the relay.

The client

zubi, for Linux (a Raspberry Pi too), macOS and Windows. Paired once with a code, it reads and changes what you shared, from a terminal, a script or an AI assistant.

The relay

Where the Mac and its clients meet, wherever each one is. It only passes encrypted messages on. Zubi’s own relay is the default, and you can run your own.

Install

On your Mac: the app

Download the latest release, unzip it and move Zubi to Applications. It needs macOS 14 or later, and is signed and notarized. Open it: it adds itself to your login items, so it starts with your Mac, and connects. macOS asks for access to Reminders, Calendar and Notes when you grant it in Settings.

On a device: the client

On Linux, a Raspberry Pi (including 32-bit Raspberry Pi OS) or a Mac, in one line:

curl -fsSL https://zubi.fa-krug.de/install.sh | sh

It picks the build for the machine, checks it against the release’s checksums and installs it in /usr/local/bin, asking for sudo only if that needs it; run it again to update. ZUBI_INSTALL_DIR=$HOME/.local/bin installs without sudo. The Raspberry Pi Zero and Pi 1 have no build.

On macOS and Linux, also with Homebrew:

brew install fa-krug/tap/zubi

On Windows, with Scoop:

scoop bucket add fa-krug https://github.com/fa-krug/scoop-bucket
scoop install fa-krug/zubi

Both keep it up to date (brew upgrade, scoop update zubi). Otherwise, or for 32-bit Raspberry Pi OS, download the archive for your system from the CLI releases (tagged cli/v…), unpack it, and put zubi on your PATH:

Linux, 64-bit ARM (Raspberry Pi 3, 4, 5 on a 64-bit OS)zubi_<version>_linux_arm64.tar.gz

With Go 1.26 or later you can also run go install github.com/fa-krug/zubi/cli/cmd/zubi@latest.

Settings

Open Settings from the menu bar icon. Each tab covers one thing:

TabWhat it does
GeneralLaunch at login, keeping the Mac awake while Zubi runs, update checks, the installed version, and Check for Updates.
ConnectionWhether your devices can reach this Mac, the first pairing code, and the relay: Zubi’s, or your own.
ClientsOne pairing code per device: add, copy, rename, renew or remove them.
ActivityEverything created or edited by your devices: when, by which client, and where.
RemindersAccess to Reminders, and which lists to share, grouped by account in their own colours.
CalendarsAccess to Calendar, and which calendars to share.
NotesAccess to Notes, and which folders to share, shown as the same tree as in Notes.
AboutVersion, links to the source and release notes, and the acknowledgements.

Choosing what to share

Each list, calendar and folder has its own setting, chosen next to it in Settings:

SettingReadingAdding and editing
Off (the default)Answers 404, as if it didn’t existAnswers 404
ReadYesRefused with 403
Read & WriteYesYes

Moving a reminder or event to another list or calendar needs that one set to Read & Write too. Zubi never deletes anything, whatever the setting. Some calendars can’t be written by anyone, such as subscribed and holiday calendars, so Read & Write isn’t offered for them. Every list, calendar and folder says whether it is writable: zubi lists, zubi calendars and zubi folders mark the read-only ones.

Changes apply to the next request; there is nothing to restart. If you create a list, calendar or folder while Settings is open, click Reload at the bottom of the tab.

Reminders

Under Settings › Reminders, click Grant Access and allow full access in the dialog. Then choose Read or Read & Write for each list. Your devices get each reminder’s title, notes, list, priority, due date and whether it is done; you can ask for one list, for what is due today, or for what is overdue. In a writable list it can add reminders, and change their title, notes, due date and priority, complete or reopen them, and move them to another writable list.

Calendars

Under Settings › Calendars, grant access the same way and choose a setting for each calendar. Events come back with title, times, location, notes and URL. A recurring event comes back once per occurrence, and one request covers at most a year. In a writable calendar Zubi can add events and edit them; for a recurring event, an edit changes only the one occurrence you name, never the whole series.

Notes

Apple Notes has no public interface, so Zubi reads it by scripting Notes.app. The first time, macOS asks whether Zubi may control Notes; allow it. Some things follow from that:

  • Notes.app is opened in the background when Zubi needs it.
  • Reading is slower than Reminders or Calendar: a large folder can take a second or two.
  • Locked notes stay locked. Zubi returns their title and dates, never their text, and never changes them.
  • In a writable folder Zubi can add notes, and change a note’s title or replace its text, given as plain text or as HTML.
  • A note’s ID is a long x-coredata:// address. Pass it as the id query parameter, URL-encoded; the command-line client does this for you.

Clients

Each device has its own pairing code, under Settings › Clients. Zubi starts with one, “Default”, whose code Settings › Connection and the menu show. Click Add Client… for another device: name it after the device, so you can tell them apart in Activity, and pair the device with its code.

A code is as secret as a password: it lets a device read what you share, and change it where you allowed that. In a client’s menu, New Code… replaces its code, and Remove… removes it; either way the device can’t reach this Mac any more until it pairs again. Every client may change the same things: what is set to Read & Write.

Activity

Settings › Activity lists the last 500 things your devices created or edited, newest first: when, which client did it, what changed, and in which list, calendar or folder. It is kept on your Mac only. Clear empties it.

The client

Status and pairing

Run zubi on its own for the connection’s status:

zubi
✓ Paired as "Kitchen Pi", through wss://relay.fa-krug.de
✓ The zubi-client service keeps the connection
✓ Your Mac answers

The first time, it asks for a pairing code, pairs with your Mac, and on Linux sets up the zubi-client service, asking for sudo: it keeps the connection, also after you log out, and every command and zubi mcp go through it. Elsewhere commands connect each time, which works just as well.

zubi pair - pairs again with a new code, for another client or another relay; zubi unpair forgets the pairing and removes the service.

Commands

CommandDoes
zubi listsShared reminder lists
zubi remindersReminders; --list, --due today|overdue, --completed
zubi calendarsShared calendars
zubi eventsEvents grouped by day; --from, --to, --calendar
zubi foldersShared Notes folders as a tree
zubi notesNotes, newest first; --folder, --limit
zubi note IDOne note’s text; --html for its HTML
zubi reminders add TITLEAdd a reminder; --list, --due, --notes, --priority
zubi reminders edit IDChange a reminder; --title, --due or --clear-due, --done, --list to move it, and more
zubi reminders done IDTick a reminder off
zubi events add TITLEAdd an event; --calendar, --start, --end, --all-day, --location, --notes, --link
zubi events edit IDChange an event, or one occurrence of a recurring one with --occurrence
zubi notes add TITLEAdd a note; --folder, and --text or --html (- reads standard input)
zubi note edit IDChange a note’s title or replace its text
zubi watchA line whenever something changes; --exec runs a command each time
zubi mcpServe Zubi to AI assistants (see below)
zubi pair CODE, zubi unpairPair again, or forget the pairing
zubi relayRun your own relay (see Your own relay)

--search (or -q) on reminders, events and notes finds items by their text. --ids on reminders and events prints each item’s ID, ready to paste into an edit command. Dates and times take YYYY-MM-DD, YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM in the computer’s own time zone, or full ISO 8601. The commands that change things work only where the list, calendar or folder is set to Read & Write. zubi help lists everything.

zubi reminders add "Buy milk" --list Groceries --due 2026-11-02
zubi reminders --list Groceries --ids
zubi reminders done <id>
zubi events add "Dentist" --calendar Personal --start 2026-11-03T09:00 --end 2026-11-03T09:30

For scripts

--json prints exactly what your Mac answered, and every failure has its own exit code. Messages go to standard error, so standard output stays clean JSON.

Exit codeMeans
0Success
1Any other error, including 403 (read-only, or access not granted on the Mac) and 409 (a locked note)
2Bad usage, or not paired yet
3Your Mac refused the code: removed or renewed
4Not found, or not shared
5Your Mac didn’t answer: asleep, Zubi not running, or the relay unreachable
 
zubi events --from 2026-11-02 --to 2026-11-06 --json
zubi reminders --due overdue --json | jq -r '.reminders[].title'

Claude Desktop and MCP

zubi mcp serves Zubi to AI assistants over the Model Context Protocol, so Claude Desktop or any MCP client can list and search your reminders, events and notes, and add or edit them where you allow it. Pair the client first, then add it to Claude Desktop’s claude_desktop_config.json:

{
  "mcpServers": {
    "zubi": { "command": "/usr/local/bin/zubi", "args": ["mcp"] }
  }
}

In Claude Code: claude mcp add zubi -- zubi mcp.

The relay

Your Mac and your devices meet at a relay, so neither has to accept connections from the internet, wherever each one is. Everything between the Mac and a device is encrypted end to end with that device’s code: the relay passes messages on and can’t read them. It does see which addresses connect, when, and how much they send.

Zubi uses its own relay, wss://relay.fa-krug.de, unless you set your own under Settings › Connection. Pairing codes name the relay, so after switching, pair your devices again.

Your own relay

A relay is zubi relay, on a machine that is always on, such as a Raspberry Pi. --install-service keeps it running as a systemd service, also after you log out; run the same command with it once.

Behind a Cloudflare Tunnel: no port forwarding, needs a domain on Cloudflare
zubi relay --behind-tls-proxy --listen 127.0.0.1:8080 --install-service
Give the tunnel a public hostname, such as relay.example.com, with service type HTTP and URL 127.0.0.1:8080; write 127.0.0.1, not localhost, which can reach another program. https://relay.example.com/health answers ok when it works. The relay’s address is wss://relay.example.com.
On a machine at home, with a port forwarded
Forward TCP port 443 on the router to the machine’s port 8443, then: 
zubi relay --listen :8443 --install-service
It gets a Let’s Encrypt certificate for your public IP address, renews it, and logs its address (journalctl -u zubi-relay). Your connection needs its own public IPv4 address, and a free DuckDNS name with --domain keeps the address when your provider changes it.
On a server or a free hosting service
On a VPS, zubi relay --install-service. On Render’s free web service, zubi relay --behind-tls-proxy; the settings are in the client’s README.

Then, on the Mac, under Settings › Connection, turn on Use my own relay, enter its wss:// address, click Apply, and pair your devices again.

Updates

The app checks GitHub for a new release once a day, starting half a minute after launch. When there is one, it shows the release notes; Update Now downloads it, checks that it is signed by the same developer, replaces the app and offers to relaunch. Later skips that version until a newer one comes out. Switch off the daily check under Settings › General; Check for Updates still works. Your settings, permissions and clients survive every update.

The client doesn’t update itself: run the install command again, or brew upgrade or scoop update zubi, whichever installed it.

Security

  • Only what you shared is served, and only what you set to Read & Write can be changed, checked on every request. Nothing is ever deleted, and a locked note is never touched.
  • Every device has its own code: 32 random bytes made on your Mac and kept in its Keychain. Remove a client or renew its code, and the old one stops working at once.
  • Everything between your Mac and a device is end-to-end encrypted with that device’s code (the Noise protocol, with fresh keys for every connection). The relay can’t read it.
  • Zubi’s server answers on your Mac only (127.0.0.1); devices reach it through the relay alone.

Found a vulnerability? Please report it privately, as described in the security policy.

Troubleshooting

Connection says “Not connected”
The message under it says why: the relay can’t be reached, or, for your own relay, what answers there instead. Zubi tries again on its own. Check that the Mac is online.
“Port 8787 on this Mac is already in use”
Another app, or a second copy of Zubi, uses the port Zubi needs. Quit it, then click Start.
zubi says your Mac doesn’t answer
The Mac has to be awake, logged in and running Zubi, with Connection green. To stop it falling asleep, switch on Keep this Mac awake while Zubi serves under Settings › General.
zubi says your Mac refused its code
The client was removed or got a new code. Copy its code from Settings › Clients and run zubi pair -.
403, “access not granted on the Mac”
Zubi lost or never got permission. Open the matching tab in Settings and click Grant Access, or allow Zubi under System Settings › Privacy & Security.
404 for a list, calendar or folder that exists
It is Off in Settings. Shared items are matched by ID or by their exact name.
403, “shared read-only”, when adding or editing
The list, calendar or folder is set to Read. Set it to Read & Write in Settings. When moving an item, the target needs Read & Write too.
Notes answers 503
Zubi may not control Notes. Allow it under System Settings › Privacy & Security › Automation › Zubi › Notes, then click Grant Access under Settings › Notes.
