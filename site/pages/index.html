Your Mac’s reminders, calendars and notes, on everything else.

Zubi is a free menu bar app that gives the Reminders lists, calendars and Notes folders you choose to your other devices: a Raspberry Pi on the wall, a Linux laptop, an AI agent. They reach your Mac from anywhere, each with its own code, end-to-end encrypted. Install it, pair a device, done.

Where you can use it

Your devices use zubi, a small client for Linux (a Raspberry Pi too), macOS and Windows. Run it once, paste the code your Mac shows, and it stays connected.

  • A Raspberry Pi display

    Put today’s events and the shopping list on a screen by the front door or the fridge, and refresh it whenever something changes.

    zubi events
zubi reminders --list Groceries
zubi watch --exec ./refresh.sh

  • AI assistants

    Give Claude Desktop or any MCP client your schedule, and, in the lists and calendars you open for writing, let it add events and tick off reminders.

    zubi mcp

  • Scripts and cron jobs

    A morning summary by email, a reminder count in your shell prompt, or a backup of a Notes folder as plain text. --json gives you exact data.

    zubi reminders --due overdue --json \
  | jq -r '.reminders[].title'

  • Linux and Windows

    Your Apple calendars and reminders in a terminal on the computers Apple doesn’t make apps for.

    zubi calendars
zubi notes --folder Recipes

Set it up in three steps

A few minutes. Nothing is shared until step two, and only what you choose.

  1. Install Zubi on your Mac

    Download Zubi, unzip it, move it to Applications and open it. It starts with your Mac from then on, connects on its own and turns green. It is signed and notarized, and keeps itself up to date.

  2. Choose what to share

    In Zubi’s Settings, under Reminders, Calendars and Notes, click Grant Access, then set each list, calendar and folder to Read, or to Read & Write where your devices may also add and edit.

  3. Pair a device

    On the device, install the client and run it. Paste the pairing code from Zubi’s menu, Copy Pairing Code.

    curl -fsSL https://zubi.fa-krug.de/install.sh | sh
zubi

What Zubi shares, and what it doesn’t

Nothing until you choose

Every list, calendar and folder starts Off, which answers as if it didn’t exist. Read shares it; only Read & Write lets your devices add and edit there. Nothing is ever deleted.

A code for each device

A device reaches your Mac only with its pairing code, which encrypts everything between the two. Remove a device in Settings, and its code stops working at once.

The relay can’t read along

Your Mac and your devices meet at a relay, which only passes encrypted messages on. Zubi’s own relay is the default; you can run your own.

No account

Nothing to sign up for. Your Mac accepts no connections from the internet: it connects out to the relay, and once a day to GitHub to check for updates, which you can switch off.
